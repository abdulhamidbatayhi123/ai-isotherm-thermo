# Corrections (Paper 3A)

Every defect in Paper 3A's design, code or text found after it was written, why it was
wrong, and what fixed it. Entries are never deleted. Numbering starts at P3A-1.

| # | Defect | Fix | How it was caught |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P3A-1** | *Our own error: the Henry-limit test checked the limit at a pressure that is not the limit.* The first harness tested q*/p against the model's K(T) at ln p = -60. For random parameters, a cooperative term with an exponent barely above 1 still contributed there, so the test failed although the limit is exact. The design also allowed cooperative exponents arbitrarily close to 1, so a "cooperative" term could imitate a Henry term at every accessible pressure. | Cooperative exponents are bounded below, g >= 1.5 (`models.PotentialIsotherm.g_min`). The test evaluates the limit at ln p = -300. `docs/DESIGN.md` now states that the exact limit does not mean the data reach the Henry regime. Caught before any real data were used. | *Found 2026-10-12 by the property test over 20 random parameter draws (seed 0 failed).* |
| **P3A-2** | *A design weakness: the first proposed model let a free network carry the temperature dependence.* Convexity and the Henry limit do not constrain how the isotherm moves with temperature. On a synthetic two-site isotherm the free network fitted the training temperatures to an RMSE below 10^-3 mol/kg, yet its step position bent with temperature and the maximum error was above 1 mol/kg at 10 K outside the training range, on both sides (`scripts/diag_tmode_extrapolation.py`, `logs/diag_tmode_extrapolation.log`). | The model's temperature dependence is now thermodynamic: an integrated van't Hoff law per term, optionally with a constant heat capacity (`t_mode="vanthoff"`, `"kirchhoff"`). The free network stays as `t_mode="nn"`, and the study will cross the shape constraint (none, penalty, hard) with the temperature structure (free, van't Hoff, characteristic curve) so that each ingredient's contribution is measured separately. Synthetic only; nothing confirmatory had run. | *Found 2026-10-13 when the synthetic recovery test failed and the diagnostic separated a fitting failure from an extrapolation failure.* |
| **P3A-3** | *Our own error: seeds that did not vary anything.* In `t_mode="vanthoff"` and `"kirchhoff"` the parameters were initialised to constants, so three "seeds" gave identical fits (`logs/diag_tmode_extrapolation.log`, the vanthoff and kirchhoff rows). A seed count would have claimed a robustness check that never happened. | The capacities, positions and heats now start from a seeded random jitter (`models.PotentialIsotherm.__init__`). The pre-registration will require every reported seed spread to come from runs whose initial parameters differ, checked in code. | *Found 2026-10-13 by reading the diagnostic log: identical digits across seeds.* |
| **P3A-4** | *Our own error: a wrong DOI in the lead's brief.* The data-inventory brief gave COF-432 as 10.1021/jacs.0c07062, written from memory. Crossref returns 404 for it. The paper is Nguyen et al., J. Am. Chem. Soc. 2020, 142, 2218, DOI 10.1021/jacs.9b13094. | The inventory uses the Crossref-verified DOI. Every DOI entering the manuscript must pass the citation gate (Crossref title match), never memory. | *Found 2026-10-13 by the inventory script's Crossref check, confirmed by the lead.* |
| **P3A-5** | *Our own error: a personal email address sent to third-party services.* The lead's agent briefs told the agents to identify themselves to Unpaywall and OpenAlex with the author's university address, and one OpenAlex call by the lead did the same. This broke the author's standing rule. | The address is removed from `data/scripts/isodb_water_inventory.py` and `data/scripts/primary_doi_check.py`. Unpaywall now gets a neutral placeholder and OpenAlex gets no address. Reported to the author at once. | *Found 2026-10-13 by the lead on reading the inventory agent's report.* |
| **P3A-6** | *Our own errors: the vector digitiser calibrated axes on the wrong labels, twice.* (i) `numeric_labels` took every text span touching the label strip, so the y axis's "0" at the plot corner was used as an x tick. On Mg-SU-102 Fig. 4a this gave an x-calibration residual of 4 pt and pressures off by up to 0.5 mbar, while recall looked plausible (0.88). (ii) Where a figure has no tick marks, label box centres were used; a centred label's box depends on glyph widths, so "10" and "12" sat about 1 pt off the single-digit spacing (Co2Cl2(BTDD) SI Fig. S8, residual 0.25 pt). | (i) A label counts only if its centre lies inside the strip (regression test `test_a_corner_label_of_the_other_axis_is_not_taken`). After the fix the vector extraction reproduces the Mg-SU-102 numeric source data: 198 of 198 points, RMS 1e-5 g/g and 0.005 mbar. (ii) Without tick marks, only labels of the modal text length calibrate the axis (residual 0.04 pt). No model had seen any digitised data. | *Found 2026-11-03 by comparing the vector extraction with numeric source data, then by reading each figure's calibration residual.* |
| **P3A-7** | *Our own errors: the first raster detector found desorption markers as adsorption points, and its first benchmark mismeasured recall.* (i) Colour blobs after a morphological opening deleted hollow markers and merged crowded markers. On Mg-SU-102 rendered at 150 to 600 dpi it recovered about half the points with errors of 3 to 6 % of the axis. (ii) The disc-template detector that replaced it reported false detections in dense figures, about 2.8 % of the axis off the curve, which is the offset of the desorption branch. A bias there moves the apparent step to lower pressure, which is the quantity that changes with temperature. (iii) The first synthetic benchmark gave three temperatures identical plateaus, so markers were completely hidden and recall measured occlusion, not the detector. | (i, ii) Template matching with the figure's own marker footprint (cut from an isolated marker, usually the legend), a white-core test, and a veto of filled detections centred on an open marker of the same colour (`digitise.raster_markers`). No physical prior (such as monotonicity) is used to filter points, because that could leak into the tests. (iii) Realistic plateau separations; recall reported over all points and over points at least 30 % visible. Remaining worst case (800 px, about 80 points per isotherm, desorption on top): 2 to 3 % false points, each within about 2.8 % of the axis; reported as such in the error model. | *Found 2026-11-03 by benchmark runs and by inspecting a crop around a false detection.* |
| **P3A-8** | *A coverage rule that took three tries, all before any fit.* The digitiser recovers only visible adsorption markers. In Hanikel 2019 SI Fig. S17 the open desorption circles cover the 10, 25 and 35 C plateaus, leaving those isotherms with only their steps. A rule was needed. (i) The first version also demanded no gap in uptake above 0.25 q_ref and a first point below 0.25 q_ref. That excluded real measurements: Co2Cl2(BTDD), KMF-1 and Mg-SU-102 have genuinely steep steps, and Mg-SU-102's lowest pressure is 20 Pa. (ii) The second version required the first point at p/psat <= 0.10. That excluded COF-432 at 25 C, whose adsorption series starts at 0.21 with 1 % of q_ref; the vector data show that nothing is hidden there. (iii) The gap test ran after the 0.90 cut, so MOF-303 at 10 C, whose last visible point is at 0.971, passed with its plateau hidden. | Final rule 5 (PREREG section 3.3): on the curve as published, no gap above 0.25 in p/psat, and a start at p/psat <= 0.10 or at uptake <= 0.10 q_ref. It flags all three occluded MOF-303 isotherms and passes the real steep steps, flat pre-step regions and pressure-limited isotherms. Each version was judged only on whether it separated occlusion from physics in the digitised data; no model had been fitted to any of it. | *Found 2026-11-03 by reading the builder's per-temperature reasons against the overlays and vector data.* |
| **P3A-9** | *Our own errors: the raster tick snapper did not do what its name says, in five ways, and the image loader mishandled transparency twice.* (i) The subpixel step looked at a fixed 5 px window, so on ticks wider than that (most figures here) it returned the eye reading unchanged; calibration residuals of exactly 0.0 px were the symptom. (ii) After a full-extent fix, a frame line 3 to 4 px thick made every column next to it look like a tick, raising residuals to 2 to 7 px. (iii) Dark meant low mean intensity, so a pure red marker (mean 0.33) near the axis captured ticks of Al-fumarate (errors up to 19 px). (iv) Longer runs were preferred, so the end ticks of the Tan panels snapped onto the neighbouring frame line or onto black data inside the frame (about 8 px). (v) The frame band was found from a whole-image fraction, which misses a frame in a multi-panel image. Separately, the loader read RGBA bytes as RGB, and then ignored a PDF soft mask (/SMask), so a transparent figure (Fathieh 2018) appeared on black. | Each tick is centred between its edges, beyond the frame line's local thickness band, on the side the axis's ticks share by majority, nearest to the reading. Dark means all three channels dark. The loader composites the soft mask onto white. Tests cover ticks 1, 3 and 7 px wide, inside and outside frames 1 and 4 px thick. Every digitised source, the dataset, the colour gate, the measured error model and the benchmark were regenerated with the final code (`scripts/regenerate_digitisation.sh`). No model had seen any digitised data. | *Found 2026-11-03 by reading calibration residuals figure by figure, tick by tick, and a pixel map of the worst tick.* |
| **P3A-10** | *Our own error: the held-out isotherm set the scale of every fit.* `study.fit_cell` passed the material's q_ref (the largest uptake over all temperatures, held-out one included) to the model as its initial capacity scale, as the loss scale and as the penalty scale. In E-low the held-out lowest temperature usually carries the largest uptake, so every model was told roughly how high the unseen isotherm goes. PREREG 3.4 defined q_ref for normalising E, which is legitimate; using it inside the fit was undeclared. The development-set tuning that was running (tuning v1, 396 fits) used this scale. | Every scale of a fit now comes from the training temperatures only: q_fit is the largest training uptake (`study.fit_cell`; test `test_fit_scale_uses_training_temperatures_only`). The material q_ref is used only to score. Tuning v1 is kept as superseded (`results/superseded_tuning_v1/`) and tuning was rerun. No confirmatory fit existed. | *Found 2026-11-08 by the Claude Science adversarial review of the draft (REVIEW_cs A3), confirmed by reading `fit_cell`.* |
| **P3A-11** | *Our own error: a failed task could be averaged away.* `analyse.e_by_material` took `v.mean()`, and pandas skips NaN, so a material with one failed task got the other task's value. Only a material where both tasks failed was ranked worst, contrary to PREREG 7.2 ("never dropped"). | A material whose E-high or E-low fit failed is a failure and is ranked worst; a missing task record stops the analysis (tests `test_one_failed_task_makes_the_material_a_failure_not_the_mean_of_the_other`, `test_a_missing_task_record_stops_the_analysis`). | *Found 2026-11-08 by the review (A1), confirmed by a unit test.* |
| **P3A-12** | *Our own error: a Henry penalty that penalised almost nothing.* `henry_penalty` measured (dq/ds - q)/q_scale at 0.01 Pa, where q and dq/ds of nearly any function are tiny, so the penalty was about zero whatever the low-pressure shape. The penalty models were therefore not given the "best chance" on the Henry limit that section 4 claimed. A first fix with a 0/0 guard of 1e-12 q_scale was itself vacuous for uptakes below about 1e-11 mol/kg (caught by the new test). | Scale-free form: the relative difference (dq/ds - q)/(\|dq/ds\| + \|q\|), zero only for a linear isotherm and 1/3 for a quadratic one, with a guard of 1e-300 (test `test_henry_penalty_is_scale_free_and_tests_linearity`). | *Found 2026-11-08 by the review (C2); the guard defect by the test written for the fix.* |
| **P3A-13** | *Our own error in the text: "only the constraints differ" was not true, and the "matched" tuning budget was not matched.* The unconstrained twins optimise a different parametrisation (linear capacities and exponents) and were tuned separately, so they could end with other term counts and step numbers than P. Each cell drew 6 configurations from grids of 2 to 60, so V-pen explored 10 % of its grid against 50 % for P, which tilted H1b against the penalty model that section 4 said would get its best chance. | Two-stage tuning (`scripts/run_dev.py`): the twins inherit the tuned structure of their hard model (V-none and V-pen from P, A-none and A-pen from A-hard, F-pen from F-none), and the penalty models tune only lambda over 5 values. Every structural cell tries min(12, grid) configurations. The training-loss gap between P and V-none is reported as an optimisation diagnostic. Section 4 now says "the same function class without the constraints, the same initial function and the same tuned structure". | *Found 2026-11-08 by the review (B3, B5).* |
| **P3A-14** | *Our own errors in the H3 rule: it could not fail, and it could fail for the wrong reason.* "Penalties do not remove violations" was decided by a Wilson lower bound above 0, which holds whenever one fit out of any number violates. Violations were counted at a threshold of exactly zero, below the data's own noise, on a grid that reached 0.95 p_sat and 20 K beyond the data, where the penalties had never been applied. The Qst-range count, which a hard model can trip near saturation, sat inside the "P in none" prediction. | H3 counts a fit as violating only if a grid isotherm falls by more than 1 % of q_ref below its running maximum, or q < -1 % q_ref, inside the penalty collocation domain; strict and extrapolated-band counts are reported beside it. Four exhaustive word classes based on the Wilson interval against 20 % (brute-force test over n up to 60). The Qst-range count is outside P's zero rule. | *Found 2026-11-08 by the review (C1); the lessons file already required thresholds above the data's floor (P3B-5).* |
| **P3A-15** | *Our own errors in the heat benchmark and its description.* PREREG 6.3a promised "RMSE of Qst(q) over loadings 0.1 to 0.9 q_ref", while `study.qst_error` compared heats at the observed (p, T) states, so a model with a displaced step was compared with the truth at a different loading. The Kirchhoff-Sips truth (dc = 40 J/mol/K) was called "inside P's family"; it is inside P-K's. All truths had heats within about 10 kJ/mol of water's condensation enthalpy. | The primary metric is loading-matched (the model's Qst at its own loading against the truth's Qst at that loading, by inverting the monotone truth); the state-matched metric is secondary; an undefined heat fails the task, as now declared. Two truths added: strong hydrophilic sites (80 kJ/mol) and a temperature-dependent capacity. Wording corrected. | *Found 2026-11-08 by the review (F1, F2, I4).* |
| **P3A-16** | *Our own errors in the freeze machinery.* The scorers for H2, the H3 words and the summary sentences did not exist, so they would have been written after the freeze. The manifest omitted the confirmatory script itself, the dataset, the digitiser, the power and world results and the environment; there was no .gitattributes and no version lock; BOMs were not handled. The power analysis was a normal approximation of the test alone (it ignored that "P better" also needs R <= 0.8 and an interval below 1), and the "can it fail" check had two worlds and one expectation that "inconclusive" satisfied. | `scripts/analyse.py` implements every rule (exact Wilcoxon and exact Walsh intervals, H1a-e with Holm over 5, H2 with Holm over 15, H3 classes, summary sentences; brute-force tests). The manifest covers 32 files plus the environment; `--prepare-freeze` writes LF files, `.gitattributes` (-text) and `requirements-lock.txt`. `scripts/mde.py` simulates the verdict itself over five spreads and two error shapes. `scripts/verdict_worlds.py` has four worlds with expectations fixed in advance. | *Found 2026-11-08 by the review (A2, D1, G1, H1).* |
| **P3A-17** | *A gate stricter than its purpose.* The colour gate required every pair of series colours in a figure to differ by at least 0.36, so one close pair excluded the whole figure, including series that were themselves well separated. Its purpose is narrower: a digitised series must not be confused with any other colour present. | A series is digitised only if it differs from every other series colour in the figure (used or not) by at least 0.36, and a figure needs 3 such temperature series; the CIE76 Delta E is reported beside the RGB distance (`scripts/check_colour_separability.py`). No decision changed: every figure that passed still passes, and the two Fathieh 2018 figures (6 of 9 series now separable) are excluded by the overlay gate, because the adsorption steps of all nine temperatures coincide and are drawn over each other. Decided on colour distances and images only, before any fit. | *Found 2026-11-08 by the review (E3).* |
| **P3A-18** | *Our own error in the vector digitiser: desorption outlines could count as adsorption points.* `markers_outside` accepted, besides filled markers of the series colour, any stroke-only item of that colour. Where an open desorption marker is drawn as a separate outline, it was taken as an adsorption point: on bnn-1-Ni Fig. 3b (a validation figure) this gave 2 false detections and one mismatched point with an error of 0.60 % of the axis. | Stroke-only items count only for a series declared hollow (`hollow=True`; no source uses it). Checked on every confirmatory vector source that uses the function (Co2Cl2(BTDD), KMF-1, PI-3-COF, NO-PI-3-COF; and MOF-801 Kim 2018): no stroke-only item lay away from a filled marker; the only ones, in Co2Cl2(BTDD), are the outlines of filled markers. Regenerating those sources gave the same points (KMF-1, both PI-3 COFs and MOF-801 byte-identical; Co2Cl2(BTDD) differs by at most 4e-4 cm3/g on a 1223 cm3/g axis, because the de-duplication now keeps the filled item instead of its outline). On bnn-1-Ni the false detections fall to 0 and the largest error to 0.085 %. | *Found 2026-11-08 by the new validation on real figures with numeric truth (review E4; `scripts/digitisation_validation.py`).* |
| **P3A-19** | *An error model that was too optimistic.* SIGMA_REL = 0.002 q_ref came from one figure (Mg-SU-102 rendered from vector, no compression). Real raster figures with deposited numbers give an RMS uptake error of 0.0012 to 0.0034 of the isotherm's own maximum. The H3 tolerance of 0.01 q_ref was set against the old value, so a model that follows the data's scatter could have been scored as violating (the floor rule, P3B-5). | SIGMA_REL = 0.004 (the largest measured value, rounded up) for the synthetic noise of H2 and the worlds; the H3 tolerance is 0.02 q_ref (five times SIGMA_REL). Both changed before any fit; the worlds, the H2 benchmark and the power simulation had not been run with the old values on any confirmatory-shaped data. | *Found 2026-11-08 by the same validation.* |
